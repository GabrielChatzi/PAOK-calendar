'''PAOK BC''' ({{langx|el|ΚΑΕ ΠΑΟΚ}}, Πανθεσσαλονίκειος Αθλητικός Όμιλος Κωνσταντινοπολιτών, ''Panthessaloníkios Athlitikós Ómilos Konstantinopolitón'', "Pan-Thessalonikian [[Sports club|Athletic Club]] of [[Constantinople|Constantinopolitans]]"), commonly known as '''PAOK''', or as '''PAOK Thessaloniki''' in European competitions, is the professional [[basketball]] department of the major [[Greece|Greek]] multi-sports club [[P.A.O.K.|A.C. PAOK]], which was founded in 1926, and is based in [[Thessaloniki]]. The club's basketball section was founded in 1928. The club's home arena is the [[PAOK Sports Arena]], which is an [[indoor arena]] with a [[seating capacity]] of 8,500 people.<ref name="PAOKTimeline">{{cite web
 |url=https://paokbc.gr/en/history/timeline
 |title=Timeline
 |website=PAOK BC
 |access-date=23 July 2026
}}</ref>

Over the years, the PAOK basketball club has established itself with a firm reputation in Greek professional basketball, especially due to its successes in European-wide competitions. In the past, the club has won Greece's top-tier level [[Greek Basket League]] twice, in 1959 and 1992. The club has also won the top national [[Greek Basketball Cup|Greek Cup]] competition three times, in 1984, 1995, and 1999.<ref name="PAOKTrophies">{{cite web
 |url=https://www.paokbc.gr/en/history/trophies
 |title=Trophies
 |website=PAOK BC
 |access-date=23 July 2026
}}</ref>

In Pan-European competitions, PAOK has also won two European Cup titles. They won the now defunct [[European professional club basketball system|European-wide secondary]] level competition, the [[FIBA Saporta Cup]], in the [[1990–91 FIBA European Cup Winners' Cup|1990–91 season]]. They also won the now defunct European-wide third level competition, the [[FIBA Korać Cup]], in the [[1993–94 FIBA Korać Cup|1993–94 season]]. In addition, PAOK was the [[FIBA Saporta Cup Finals]]' runner-up in both the [[1991–92 FIBA European Cup|1991–92]] and [[1995–96 FIBA European Cup|1995–96]] seasons. On the European-wide top-tier level, PAOK competed in the [[EuroLeague]]'s concluding championship tournament, the [[EuroLeague Final Four|Final Four]], at the [[1993 EuroLeague Final Four|1993 Athens EuroLeague Final Four]], where they finished in third place.

==History==

===1928–1980===
[[File:PAOK vs HANTH.jpg|thumb|left|180px|PAOK vs. [[YMCA Thessaloniki BC|HANTH (YMCA)]] during the 1920s]]

The men's basketball section of the multi-sports club [[PAOK AC]] was created in 1928, when Alekos Alexiadis, a young member of the administration council of PAOK AC (founded in 1926), took the initiative to create a men's basketball team. He "gave birth" to the basketball department again, after [[World War II]]. After the war, Alekos Alexiadis began to organize a basketball team from the children that played at the only basketball court in [[Thessaloniki]]. The first honor for PAOK's basketball section was the win of the 1958–59 [[Greek Basket League|Greek League]] championship. The team was crowned the Champions of Greece, with the following players; Dapontes, Kyriakou, [[Giorgos Oikonomou|Oikonomou]], Paschalis, Stalios, Kokkos, Theoridis, Angelidis, Stergiou, Konstantinidis, and [[player-coach]] Irakleios Klagas. The next year, PAOK competed for the first time in a European-wide competition and was eliminated in the first round of the [[EuroLeague|European Champions Cup]] by the Romanian champions [[CSA Steaua București (basketball)|CCA București]]. Three years later, the Greek League was reconstructed into a Nation-wide League, but PAOK was placed in the [[Greek B Basket League|Second National Division]], where they won an immediate [[Promotion and relegation|league promotion]] to the first division the next year. The worst season of the club to date was in 1976–77, when PAOK avoided [[relegation]], with a 66–53 play-out win over Dimokritos.

===1981–1990===
PAOK met [[Panathinaikos B.C.|Panathinaikos]] in its first [[Greek Basketball Cup|Greek Cup]] Final, in 1982. The Athenians managed to scrape through to a two-point victory, despite the game being played in [[Alexandreio Melathron]], which was PAOK's home arena at that time. In the following 1982–83 season, the team finished in second place behind [[Aris B.C.|Aris]].

The success of both Aris and PAOK fuelled the ongoing rivalry between the clubs, which had long been established in football. In 1984, the two teams reached the Greek Cup Final. PAOK's head coach, [[Faidon Matthaiou]], ordered the players to shave their heads in an attempt to strengthen the team's morale. PAOK won the Greek Cup 74–70, in what became known as the "final of the shaven heads".<ref name="PAOKTimeline" />

The summer of 1984 also marked an important administrative turning point for the club, as [[Nikolaos Vezyrtzis|Nikos Vezyrtzis]] took over the leadership of PAOK's basketball department. During his administration, the club strengthened its roster and gradually developed from a domestic contender into one of the leading teams in Greek and European basketball.<ref name="Vezyrtzis1984">{{cite web
 |url=https://www.paokbc.gr/en/player/162
 |title=PAOK's first foreign players
 |website=PAOK BC
 |access-date=9 August 2026
}}</ref>

The then 22-year-old [[Bane Prelević]], debuted with the club in the 1988–89 season. He became the definitive leader of PAOK, and a fan favorite. He was often compared to the great [[Nikos Galis]], who was at the time the [[captain (sports)|captain]] of Aris. Prelević was often quoted for his loyalty to the team. He had a number of injuries and medical emergencies because of weak legs, but he would constantly choose to take heavy dosages of painkillers, rather than missing out on important games. During the 1980s decade, PAOK was second in the Greek League only to Aris.

===1991–2000===
PAOK won the now defunct [[European professional club basketball pyramid|European second-tier]] level [[FIBA Saporta Cup|FIBA European Cup Winners' Cup]]'s ([[Saporta Cup]]'s) [[1990–91 FIBA European Cup Winners' Cup|1990–91 season]] championship, when PAOK defeated the [[Spanish ACB League]] club [[CB Zaragoza|CAI Zaragoza]] in [[Geneva]], by a score of 76–72, on March 26, 1991.

In the following [[1991–92 FIBA European Cup|1991–92 season]], PAOK once again reached [[FIBA Saporta Cup Finals|the final]] of the same competition (then called the [[FIBA Saporta Cup|FIBA European Cup]]), but they lost to the Spanish League club [[Real Madrid Baloncesto|Real Madrid Asegurator]], by a score of 65–63. The game was heading to [[overtime (sports)|overtime]], as the two teams were equal at 63 points, when [[Panagiotis Fasoulas]] lost the ball to [[Rickey Brown]] in the last 2 seconds of the game, and Real Madrid scored an unexpected basket. The same year, PAOK won the [[Greek Basket League|Greek League]] championship, by beating [[Aris B.C.|Aris]] in the league's final four mini stage, and then [[Olympiacos B.C.|Olympiacos]] in the playoff's finals.

In the [[1992–93 FIBA European League|1992–93 season]], PAOK had a starting five unit of [[Jon Korfas]], [[Bane Prelević]], [[Cliff Levingston]], [[Ken Barlow (basketball)|Ken Barlow]] and [[Panagiotis Fasoulas]], and was led by head coach [[Dušan Ivković]]. In that season, the club competed in Europe's premier club basketball competition, the [[FIBA European League]] (now called [[EuroLeague]]), which marked the club's first appearance in the top-tier level European league. PAOK reached the [[1993 FIBA European League Final Four|1993 Athens Final Four]]. PAOK lost in the semifinal game to the then defending [[Lega Basket Serie A|Italian League]] champions [[Pallacanestro Treviso|Benetton Treviso]], which was led by the Croatian star [[Toni Kukoč]], by a score of 79–77. Two days later, in the third-place game, PAOK defeated Spain's [[Real Madrid Baloncesto|Real Madrid Teka]], which was led by the Lithuanian star [[Arvydas Sabonis]] and American [[Rickey Brown]].

The 1993 Final Four marked the end of Vezyrtzis' first period at the head of PAOK's basketball department. During his tenure, PAOK had established itself among the leading clubs in European basketball, highlighted by the 1991 European Cup Winners' Cup and the 1992 Greek championship. Following the 1992–93 season, Vezyrtzis stepped down and handed control of the basketball department to Apostolos Oikonomidis.<ref name="Vezyrtzis1993">{{cite web
 |url=https://www.paokbc.gr/en/player/117

 |website=PAOK BC

}}</ref>

In the [[1993–94 FIBA Korać Cup|1993–94 season]], PAOK returned to European success, by winning the European third-tier level [[FIBA Korać Cup]] competition, in a two-legged final against the Italian League club [[Pallacanestro Trieste|Stefanel Trieste]]. PAOK won both at home and away, by 9 points. The following year, PAOK won the [[Greek Basketball Cup|Greek Cup]] title, in a 19-point victory against [[Panionios B.C.|Chipita Panionios]], by a score of 72–53. In the [[1995–96 FIBA European Cup|1995–96 season]], PAOK once again reached the final of the [[FIBA Saporta Cup|FIBA European Cup]] (Saporta Cup), but they lost to the Spanish club [[Saski Baskonia|Taugrés]], by a score of 88–81. Three years later in 1999, PAOK again won the Greek Cup title, by defeating [[AEK B.C.|AEK Athens]], by a score of 71–54.

The new home of PAOK, the [[PAOK Sports Arena]], able to hold 8,500 fans, hosted its first official PAOK game on 17 May 2000, when the club faced [[Panathinaikos B.C.|Panathinaikos]] in the second game of the Greek League Finals.<ref name="PAOKArena2000">{{cite web |url=https://www.paokbc.gr/el/nea/special/996-20-paok-sports-arena |title=Τα 20 χρόνια του ΠΑΟΚ στο PAOK Sports Arena με αριθμούς… |website=PAOK BC |date=18 May 2020 |language=el |access-date=9 August 2026}}</ref> The arena's construction had been a long-standing project in which Vezyrtzis played a key role, strongly promoting the creation of a permanent basketball home for PAOK on land donated by the Dedeoglou family.<ref name="VezyrtzisArena">{{cite web |last=Sountoulidis |first=Stavros |url=https://www.gazzetta.gr/stili/oi-prwtagwnistes/article/838128/vezyrtzis-mia-zoi-paok |title=Βεζυρτζής: Μια ζωή ΠΑΟΚ! |website=Gazzetta |date=27 November 2015|language=el |access-date=9 August 2026}}</ref> The move marked the end of a long period in which the club had shared the home court of [[Alexandreio Melathron]] with [[Aris B.C.|Aris]].

Bane Prelević returned to PAOK, after having quick spells at [[Virtus Pallacanestro Bologna|Kinder Bologna]] and [[AEK B.C.|AEK Athens]], and then quit playing basketball at the end of the 1999–00 season. He later returned to PAOK in the 2001–02 season, as an [[basketball coach|assistant coach]].

During the 1990s decade, PAOK was one of the top teams in the Greek League. They won the Greek League championship in 1992, and also played in the league's finals in 1994, 1998, and 2000. In addition to that, PAOK also made it to the league's semifinals each year, and they finished in 3rd place in the Greek league 1993, 1997, and 1999.

===2001–2010===
PAOK played in [[Euroleague Basketball|EuroLeague Basketball]]'s inaugural [[2000–01 Euroleague|2000–01 season]], where they made it to the Round of 16, and lost to the [[Slovenian Basketball League|Slovenian League]] club [[KK Olimpija|Union Olimpija]]. However, a difficult financial situation led the previous season's [[Greek Basket League|Greek League]] finalists to struggle in the Greek League, where they finished in 8th place in the [[2000–01 Greek Basket League|2000–01 Greek League]] season. In the [[2001–02 Greek Basket League|2001–02 season]], PAOK again finished in 8th place in the Greek League. [[Bane Prelević|Prelević]] became the team's [[head coach]] for the [[2002–03 Greek Basket League|2002–03 season]], and he led them to a short winning streak during the year. PAOK ended up in 7th place in the Greek League in the 2002–03 season. PAOK also competed in [[FIBA Europe|FIBA]]-organized Pan-European club competitions at that time. They competed in the European third-level [[2001–02 FIBA Korać Cup]], and in the European third-level [[2002–03 FIBA Europe Champions Cup]] ([[EuroChallenge]]).

PAOK finished in 6th place in the [[2003–04 Greek Basket League|2003–04 Greek League season]], with a squad full of talented young players, like [[Panos Vasilopoulos]], [[Kostas Vasileiadis]] and [[Loukas Mavrokefalidis]]. During the season, PAOK withdrew from FIBA-run competitions, and in the [[2004–05 ULEB Cup|2004–05 season]], the team made its debut in the European-wide secondary level [[ULEB Cup]] ([[EuroCup Basketball|EuroCup]]) competition. PAOK made it to the ULEB Cup's quarterfinals that season, where they lost to the [[Lithuanian Basketball League|Lithuanian League]] club [[BC Lietuvos rytas|Lietuvos Rytas]]. In the Greek League, PAOK finished in 6th place. In the [[2005–06 Greek Basket League|2005–06 season]], PAOK finished in 6th place in the Greek League.

During a [[2006–07 Greek Basket League|2006–07 Greek League season]] game, PAOK and [[Aris BC|Aris]] put on a spectacular show, that PAOK ended up winning, after two [[overtime (sports)|overtime]] periods.<ref>[http://contra.gr/Basketball/Hellas/A1Basket/PAOK_Aris/136592.html PAOK-Aris 101–99. {{in lang|el}}]</ref> The team finished that season's Greek League in 6th place, after losing to [[Olympiacos B.C.|Olympiacos]] in the league's playoffs. PAOK also played in the ULEB Cup (EuroCup) in the [[2006–07 ULEB Cup|2006–07 season]]. They made it to the league's Top 16 Round that season, where they lost to the [[Lega Basket Serie A|Italian League]] club [[Mens Sana 1871 Basket|Montepaschi Siena]].

The [[2007–08 Greek Basket League|2007–08 Greek League]] season was one of the worst in PAOK's history, with PAOK finishing in a disastrous 12th place in the league. However, during that Greek League season, PAOK picked up a great victory against Olympiacos. In that same [[2007–08 FIBA EuroCup|2007–08 season]], PAOK also had a disappointing finish in the now defunct European-wide third- level competition, the [[FIBA EuroChallenge|FIBA EuroCup]] ([[EuroChallenge]]). Because of that, PAOK's management decided for the club to take a break from Pan-European leagues, and to instead focus mainly on the Greek League. So the club did not compete in any European-wide leagues in the following 2008–09 and 2009–10 seasons.

PAOK finished in 7th place in the [[2008–09 Greek Basket League|2008–09 Greek League season]]. Prior to the start of the 2009–10 season, PAOK hired [[Soulis Markopoulos]] to be the team's new head coach. PAOK finished in 5th place in the [[2009–10 Greek Basket League|2009–10 Greek League season]].

===2010–2020===
PAOK finished 3rd place in the Greek League in the [[2010-11 Greek Basket League|2010–11 season]]. That success allowed PAOK to play in the [[EuroLeague]]'s qualifying tournament in the [[2011-12 Euroleague|2011–12 season]]. However, in the [[2011-12 Greek Basket League|2011–12 season]], PAOK finished in a disappointing 8th place in the Greek League, and thus missed out on European-wide competition for the 2012–13 season. In the [[2012-13 Greek Basket League|2012–13 season]], PAOK finished in 5th place in the Greek League, and was then able to return to European competition for the following season. In the next season, they competed in the 2nd-tier level [[EuroCup Basketball|EuroCup]]. In the Greek League, PAOK finished in 3rd place, in both the [[2013-14 Greek Basket League|2013–14]] and [[2014–15 Greek Basket League|2014–15]] seasons. In the [[2015-16 Greek Basket League|2015–16 season]], PAOK finished in 5th place in the Greek League. After the [[2015–17 FIBA–Euroleague Basketball controversy]], PAOK decided to return to [[FIBA Europe|FIBA]] organized competitions, and chose to play in the [[FIBA Champions League]], rather than in the EuroCup. After finishing in 5th place in the Greek League in the [[2016–17 Greek Basket League|2016–17 season]], the club replaced head coach [[Soulis Markopoulos]] with [[Ilias Papatheodorou]]. In the [[2017-18 Greek Basket League|2017–18 season]], PAOK finished in 3rd place in the Greek League. The [[2019–20 Greek Basket League|2019–20 season]] was the worst on PAOK history because PAOK finished in the last place of Greek basketball league; but due to [[COVID-19 pandemic]], it wasn't relegated.

In May 2026, PAOK announced the development of a private basketball training centre on an 8,000-square-metre site in [[Pylaia]], Thessaloniki. The project was scheduled for completion in autumn 2026.<ref name="TrainingCentre">{{cite web

|website=PAOK BC
|date=8 May 2026
|access-date=23 July 2026
}}</ref>

The facilities are planned to include:
* Two fully equipped indoor basketball courts
* Individual and team training areas
* A strength and rehabilitation gym
* Locker rooms for athletes and coaching staff
* A game analysis and educational activities room
* Medical and physiotherapy facilities
* Outdoor green areas and parking spaces

==Management==

Alpha Sports Group Single Member S.A., owned by [[Aristotelis Mistakidis]] (60.81%)

On 17 December 2025, businessman [[Aristotelis Mistakidis]], through Alpha Sports Group Single Member S.A., acquired a controlling 60.81% stake in PAOK B.C. The company purchased shares previously held by PAOK B.C. president Thanasis Hatzopoulos (31.61%), Vangelis Galatsopoulos (23.77%) and Ilias Violidis (5.43%). Mistakidis is a prominent metals trader, has been a major shareholder of [[Piraeus Bank]] since 2021, and is the controlling shareholder of Aegean Baltic Bank.<ref>[https://www.newmoney.gr/roh/palmos-oikonomias/trapezes/o-telis-mistakidis-apokta-ton-elegcho-tis-aegean-baltic-bank Aristotelis Mistakidis acquires control of Aegean Baltic Bank]</ref>
